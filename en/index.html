Why firefighter fitness?

Emergency operations demand endurance, strength, speed, coordination and agility – often with breathing apparatus and in full protective gear. Yet there are still few standardized training programs and uniform fitness tests; both are often left to individual departments.

01 · Demands

Firefighting demands peak performance

Internationally, overexertion is considered the leading cause of on-duty deaths and of musculoskeletal injuries.

02 · Health

Fitness cannot be taken for granted

Studies show insufficient fitness, overweight and cardiovascular risk factors in many firefighters; back pain is common.

03 · Practice

A lack of common standards

The aim of the project: evidence-based training and time-efficient testing that work in the daily routine of a fire station.

The training program

Every exercise is linked to a firefighting task

Two circuits (A and B), each with eight functional exercises performed as intervals: together they target all major muscle groups and provide variety. Short, intense, requiring little equipment and scalable to any fitness level.

Level 1 · EntryManuscript in preparation

Low-threshold preparatory training

For firefighters with low fitness or returning after a break: a gentle re-entry that leads up to HIFT. Without time pressure, preparatory movement patterns and whole-body exercises are taught with an emphasis on correct technique – to improve safety and prevent injuries. Tested in the project “Fit fürs Feuer” with new recruits of the Göttingen professional fire department.

Level 2 · Main programPublished

HIFT circuit for firefighters

The core of the project: High-Intensity Functional Training that addresses several job-relevant abilities in one session – aerobic and anaerobic capacity, strength and muscular endurance. The exercises mimic the muscular demands of typical firefighting tasks, from stair climbing to forcible entry.

Shown: circuit A. Circuit B varies the exercises, e.g. lat pull-down instead of rows or push-ups instead of overhead press.

Next step · 2027

The intervention study

Together with a professional fire department, we will examine how the HIFT program affects the physical performance of firefighters.

  1. 1
    Baseline testing
    Field test battery from step 3b, carried out directly at the fire station.
  2. 2
    Training phase
    First 4 weeks of low-threshold training as preparation, then HIFT once or twice a week for 6–8 weeks (shorter if agreed with the department), supervised and scaled.
  3. 3
    Final testing & analysis
    Pre/post comparison; results are shared with participants and the fire station.

What’s in it for the fire department

  • a tested training concept that needs little equipment
  • a fitness profile for every participating firefighter
  • a testing routine that fits into the daily routine of a fire station
  • a results report and presentation on request

Poster

Verletzungen und Beschwerden bei Teilnehmenden des Toughest Firefighter Alive Wettkampfes [Injuries and complaints among participants of the Toughest Firefighter Alive competition]

Sempf, F. & Thienes, G. · Sports, Medicine and Health Summit 2023

71 injuries in the preceding twelve months among 69 competitors; ligament and muscle injuries were most common, the knee was most frequently affected. Few injuries during the competition itself.

2023
Poster

Modified Star Excursion Balance Test Scores of Firefighters

Sempf, F. & Thienes, G. · Sports, Medicine and Health Summit 2023

Firefighter-specific reference values for the mSEBT in 49 professional firefighters. Notable: lower anterior reach and a side-to-side asymmetry of at least 10% in 36.7%. Balance tests are therefore recommended for fire departments.

Sempf, F. & Thienes, G. · Sports, Medicine and Health Summit 2021 · Dtsch Z Sportmed, 72

Heart rate and blood lactate in 39 participants of the 2018 open TFA European Championship were consistently in the “very hard” intensity range. The values show what is possible in job-specific tasks under maximal competitive exertion.
